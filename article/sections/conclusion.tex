\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This work asked to what extent the structural characteristics of a Brazilian high school predict
the average ENEM score of its students. On 23{,}374 schools and under a protocol built to avoid
inflated error estimates (cross-validation grouped by municipality, identical repeated partitions
for all algorithms, and nested tuning), they predict a large share of it. A tuned gradient boosting
model reaches an RMSE of 30.6 points ($R^2 = 0.76$), against 62.4 points when predicting the mean
and 39.7 points when only the administrative network is known. Gradient boosting, the SVR, and the
random forest are within 0.3 RMSE points of each other, so the choice among them matters much less
than the evaluation protocol and the features.

Most of this predictive power is tied to whether the school is public or private. The
administrative network dominates the SHAP values, and the school-meal indicator acts as a second
proxy for it. Without the network, gradient boosting loses little ($R^2 = 0.73$), because it
reconstructs it from the other attributes; without the network and the school meals, the
remaining structural attributes still explain $R^2 = 0.64$, against 0.50 for linear regression.
The errors are also not uniform: federal schools are underpredicted in the Southeast and South and
overpredicted in the North and Center-West, which suggests that a single national effect of the
network hides regional differences.

As discussed in Section~\ref{sec:interp-limitations}, these results describe associations, not
causes. The analysis is at
the school level and does not describe individual students, and it covers only schools with at
least ten candidates present in all ENEM tests. It also relies on a single exam year and on the
2024 census, one year before the exam; the schools with no high-school enrolment in the census
(253) or with more candidates than enrolments (276) show that the lag is real, although its effect
should be small for slowly changing structural attributes. Some selected hyperparameters lie on the border of the grids, so the tuned
models might still improve. Finally, the p-values of the statistical tests are optimistic because
the cross-validation measurements are not independent.

Future work could add an indicator of the socioeconomic level of the students, to separate the
contribution of the school structure from that of its students; repeat the study over several
ENEM editions, with the census of the same year and a temporal validation; and study the schools
that perform well above what their structure predicts, which are natural candidates for
identifying effective practices.
